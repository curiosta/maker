What the official assignment asks for

Two class pages share this lecture date: applications and implications (plan the final project) and project development (summary slide and video drafts).

Individual assignment

  • Plan a final project masterpiece that integrates the range of units covered: what it will do, who has done what beforehand, what sources you will use, what you will design, what materials and components it needs, where they come from and what they cost, what parts and systems you will make, what processes you will use, what questions need answering, and how it will be evaluated.
  • The project should incorporate 2D and 3D design, additive and subtractive fabrication, electronics design and production, embedded microcontroller design, interfacing and programming, and system integration and packaging.
  • Where possible, make rather than buy the parts. Projects can be separate or joint but need to show individual mastery and be independently operable.
  • Project development: prepare drafts of your final project summary slide (presentation.png, 1920x1080) and video clip (presentation.mp4, 1080p).

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Define the scope of a project.
  • Develop a project plan with a schedule and a bill of materials.
  • Track the progress of your project.
  • Summarise and communicate the essence of your project.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • The slide is presentation.png (1920x1080) and the video presentation.mp4 (1080p, about a minute, about 25 MB at most), both in the root of your site.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • What will it do?
  • Who has done what beforehand?
  • What sources will you use?
  • What will you design?
  • What materials and components will you use, and where will they come from?
  • How much will they cost? (a draft bill of materials is enough)
  • What parts and systems will you make, and with what processes?
  • What questions need answering, and how will it be evaluated?
  • Uploaded a draft summary slide and video clip, and checked they are linked from the final presentation schedule.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • Every planning question answered in writing.
  • A first bill of materials with sources, even if incomplete.
  • A schedule for the remaining weeks.
  • Draft presentation.png and presentation.mp4 in the right place.

Exceptional

  • Prior work researched and credited, with how yours differs.
  • A risk list with a fallback for each risky part.
  • Evaluation criteria you can actually test.
  • A project that clearly uses 2D and 3D design, additive and subtractive processes, your own electronics and an interface.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Leaving the bill of materials until the end.
  • Placeholder slide and video not linked or in the wrong folder.
  • A plan with no dates.
  • A scope too big for the weeks left.
