What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Probe an input device's analog levels and digital signals.

Individual assignment

  • Measure something: add a sensor to a microcontroller board that you have designed and read it.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate workflows for sensing something with input devices and a microcontroller board.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • The group work should at least use a multimeter and an oscilloscope.
  • No breadboards: the sensor goes on a board you designed.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Documented what you learned interfacing an input device, and optionally how the physical property relates to the readings.
  • Documented your board's design and fabrication, or linked to the board you made earlier.
  • Explained how your code works.
  • Explained problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included original design files and source code.
  • Included a hero shot of your board.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A sensor read by a board you designed and made, with readings shown (serial output or a plot).
  • The group's multimeter and oscilloscope measurements, with your contribution clear.
  • Your code explained, and the design files and source code included.

Exceptional

  • Readings calibrated against a known reference, with the relationship between the physical quantity and the numbers explained.
  • Noise looked at and handled (filtering, averaging, sampling rate), with before and after data.
  • More than one kind of sensor compared, for example analog versus digital.
  • The sensor chosen and placed the way your final project needs.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Using a breadboard.
  • Printing raw numbers with no units or meaning.
  • Reading a sensor at the wrong voltage level for your microcontroller. Check the datasheet.
  • Skipping the oscilloscope in the group work.

  • MicroPython ADCanalog input in MicroPython
  • PulseView (sigrok)decode digital sensor signals
  • Arduino Wire (I2C)many digital sensors use I2C
