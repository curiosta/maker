What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Do your lab's safety training.
  • Characterize your lasercutter's focus, power, speed, rate, kerf, joint clearance and types.

Individual assignment

  • Cut something on the vinylcutter.
  • Design, lasercut and document a parametric construction kit, accounting for the lasercutter kerf.

Extra credit (optional, as marked on the official page)

  • Design it to be assembled in multiple ways.
  • Include elements that are not flat.
  • Engrave as well as cut.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate and describe parametric 2D modelling.
  • Identify and explain the processes involved in using the laser cutter.
  • Develop, evaluate and build a parametric construction kit.
  • Identify and explain the processes involved in using the vinyl cutter.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • 'Characterize' means describing the laser cutter's features and key settings.
  • The Inkscape clone tool does not count as parametric design.
  • A construction kit is more than a finger-joint box: it can be assembled in several ways, holds together without glue, and does not fall apart when lifted.
  • You may vinyl-cut a design that is not yours, but you must say where it came from.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Reflected, on your own page, on what you learned in your lab's safety training.
  • Explained how you created your parametric design.
  • Documented how you made your press-fit construction kit.
  • Documented how you made something with the vinyl cutter.
  • Included your original design files.
  • Included hero shots of your results.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A kerf and joint-clearance test, with the measured values used in your design.
  • A parametric press-fit kit where slot width and material thickness are variables, assembled in more than one way, holding without glue.
  • A vinyl cut, with its source credited if you did not draw it.
  • A paragraph on the safety training and the original files linked.

Exceptional

  • The official extras: assembled in many ways, non-flat elements, engraving as well as cutting.
  • The same kit regenerated for a different material thickness, showing the parameters really work.
  • A vinyl cut that is applied for a purpose (a stencil, a flexible circuit, a label for your project).
  • A clear record of settings that failed and what you changed.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Using a fixed slot width instead of one tied to material thickness and kerf.
  • A kit that is really one box.
  • Not measuring the actual material thickness; sheet stock varies.
  • Leaving the laser unattended. Follow your lab's rules.
