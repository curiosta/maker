What the official assignment asks for

The schedule lists 15 Apr 2026 as a break and midterm review, between modules 13 and 14.

Group assignment

  • Review the safety data sheets for each of your molding and casting materials, then make and compare test casts with each of them.
  • Compare mold making processes.

Individual assignment

  • Design a mold around the process you'll be using, produce it with a smooth surface finish that does not show the production process toolpath, and use it to cast parts.

Extra credit (optional, as marked on the official page)

  • Use more than two mold parts.
  • Make your own materials.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Design objects suited to the limits of your process.
  • Demonstrate workflows used in mold design, construction and casting.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • The group work is for getting to know materials and processes; you do not make a mold there.
  • Always read the safety and technical data sheets, and write down the main points.
  • A single negative mold is fine if the part can be cast in it without breaking the mold.
  • A 3D-printed mold is fine if the surface is smooth; FDM prints need post-processing.
  • For the assignment the mold must be milled or 3D printed; you can explore other ways afterwards.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page and reflected on what you learned.
  • Reviewed the safety data sheets for each material, and made and compared test casts with each.
  • Documented how you designed and made your mold, including machine settings.
  • A mold with a smooth surface that does not show the toolpath (post-processed if needed).
  • Shown how you safely made your mold and cast the parts.
  • Described problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included design files and a hero shot of the mold and the final object.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A milled or printed mold with a smooth finish, designed around your casting material.
  • Parts cast from it, with photos of the mold and the result.
  • The safety data sheet points written down, with the protective equipment you used.
  • Machine settings, problems and fixes recorded.

Exceptional

  • The official extras: more than two mold parts, or your own materials.
  • Draft angles, vents and registration keys designed in and explained.
  • A test of mix ratio, degassing or cure time, with results compared.
  • A cast part that is used in your final project.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Visible toolpath lines on the mold, which transfer to the cast.
  • No draft or sharp undercuts, so the part will not come out.
  • Wrong mix ratio or not weighing the parts.
  • Skipping gloves, eye protection or ventilation the data sheet asks for.
