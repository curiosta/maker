What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Test the design rules for your 3D printer(s).

Individual assignment

  • Design, document and 3D print an object that could not be made subtractively (small, a few cm³, limited by printer time).
  • 3D scan an object (and optionally print it).

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Identify the advantages and limitations of 3D printing.
  • Apply design methods and production processes to show you understand 3D printing.
  • Demonstrate how scanning can digitize objects.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • 'Not easily made subtractively' means features such as undercuts, overhangs, print-in-place or nested parts that cannot be taken apart.
  • You do not need to include the scan file, which can be very large; screenshots of the process and result are fine.
  • Cleaning up the scan is not required, but it is useful to learn.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Explained what you learned from testing the 3D printers.
  • Documented how you designed and printed your object, and why it could not easily be made subtractively.
  • Documented how you scanned an object.
  • Included your original design files for printing.
  • Included your hero shots.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • Group work: a test print that checks design rules for your printer (for example clearances, overhangs, bridges, thin walls or tolerances), with the slicer settings, photos, measured results and the rules you will now follow.
  • An object that could not be made subtractively (for example internal cavities or interlocking moving parts), with CAD files, slicer settings and photos of the print.
  • A written argument for why the object cannot be made subtractively.
  • A 3D scan of an object: the method you used, the resulting mesh, and any problems you hit.
  • Printing the scan is optional in the assignment; if you skip it, say so.

Exceptional

  • The group test results are visibly used in your own design (for example a clearance you measured), not just reported.
  • Print preparation is deliberate: orientation and support choices compared, with photos of the outcomes, including failed prints and what you changed.
  • The scan is cleaned up (mesh repair), checked against real measurements of the object, and printed.
  • Parametric or editable CAD in open or widely readable formats, so others can modify it.
  • A link to your final project: where this part or technique will be used.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • A shape that a mill could make just as well, such as a simple box or extrusion.
  • Reporting group test results without using them in your own design.
  • No slicer settings recorded.
  • Scanning a shiny or featureless object and giving up; matte spray or added texture often helps photogrammetry.
