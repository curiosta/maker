What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Measure the power consumption of an output device.

Individual assignment

  • Add an output device to a microcontroller board you've designed, and program it to do something.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate workflows for controlling output devices with a microcontroller board you designed.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • A single simple LED is not enough; use an LED array, an RGB LED, a high-power LED or another output device.
  • No breadboards.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Documented how your group measured an output device's power consumption.
  • Documented what you learned interfacing and controlling the output device.
  • Linked to the board you made earlier, or documented the design and fabrication of a new one.
  • Explained how your code works.
  • Explained problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included original source code and any new design files.
  • Included a hero shot of your board.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • An output device beyond a simple LED (for example a motor, speaker, display or addressable LEDs) driven by your own board.
  • Power consumption measured in the group, with the method and numbers shown.
  • Code explained, with source and design files included.

Exceptional

  • A driver stage designed for the load (for example a MOSFET or H-bridge) with the current and voltage choices explained.
  • A power budget for your final project based on the measurement.
  • Smooth control, such as PWM ramps or a display refreshed without flicker, with what you changed to get it.
  • Protection thought through, such as a flyback diode for inductive loads.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Driving a motor or high-current load straight from a microcontroller pin.
  • No common ground between the board and an external supply.
  • Power measured only on paper, not with a meter or supply reading.
  • Using a breadboard.

Open tools and references

  • Arduino analogWrite (PWM)dimming and speed control
  • Adafruit NeoPixel Uberguideaddressable LEDs, wiring and power
  • Falstad circuit simulatortry a driver circuit first
