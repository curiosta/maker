What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Do your lab's safety training.
  • Test runout, alignment, fixturing, speeds, feeds, materials and toolpaths for your machine.

Individual assignment

  • Make (design, mill and assemble) something big (about meter-scale).

Extra credit (optional, as marked on the official page)

  • Don't use fasteners or glue.
  • Include curved surfaces.
  • Use three-axis toolpaths.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate 2D design development for CNC milling.
  • Describe workflows and operation for large-format CNC machining.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • 'Big' means working from a board at least 4 ft x 4 ft (1200 mm x 1200 mm), with nested parts of roughly 1 m x 1 m.
  • It does not have to be wood, although the lab only has to provide a full-size wood board.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Reflected, on your own page, on what you learned in your lab's safety training.
  • Documented how you designed your object and made the CAM toolpath.
  • Documented how you milled and assembled it, including machine setup, fixturing, feeds and speeds.
  • Described problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included your design files and a hero shot.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A meter-scale object designed with your measured material thickness and joint clearance.
  • The CAM settings (tool, feeds, speeds, step-down, tabs or fixturing) written down.
  • Photos of milling and assembly, and of the finished object in use.
  • Problems and fixes described, and the safety training reflected on.

Exceptional

  • The official extras: no fasteners or glue, curved surfaces, three-axis toolpaths.
  • Test cuts of your joints before the full sheet.
  • Good nesting that uses the sheet well, with offcuts planned.
  • A parametric design that adapts to a different sheet thickness.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Designing for the nominal sheet thickness instead of measuring it.
  • Forgetting dogbones or other relief in inside corners for a round cutter.
  • Parts moving during the cut because of poor fixturing or no tabs.
  • Running the machine without the lab's safety training or supervision.
