What the official assignment asks for

Version control, building and publishing your documentation website, and project management principles.

Individual assignment

  • Download, read and sign the student agreement, and commit the file to your repo.
  • Work through a git tutorial.
  • Build a personal site in the class archive describing you and your final project.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Identify and use version control.
  • Explore and use website development tools.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • A site template is acceptable if you acknowledge it and modify it.
  • Your documentation must be hosted in the class archive, not elsewhere.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • A website, with a description of how you made it.
  • A section about yourself.
  • The steps you followed to set up your Git repository and add files to it.
  • Your work pushed to your class GitLab repository.
  • The student agreement signed and uploaded.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A working site in your class repository with an about page and a final project page.
  • Your Git setup written as steps someone could follow, including how you push.
  • The signed student agreement committed to the repository.
  • If you used a template or generator, it is named and credited.

Exceptional

  • An image and video compression step built into your workflow, so the repository stays small all term.
  • A clear page structure and navigation that will hold 20 weeks of work.
  • A short note on how you will plan your weeks (for example spiral development or time-boxing), and what you will cut if time runs short.
  • Commit messages that tell the story of your changes.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Committing large, uncompressed photos and video. Repository size grows quickly and is hard to shrink later.
  • Building the site somewhere outside the class archive.
  • Leaving the about page or final project page empty.
  • Skipping the student agreement.
