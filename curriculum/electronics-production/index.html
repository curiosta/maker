What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Characterize the design rules for your in-house PCB production process.
  • Submit a PCB design to a board house.

Individual assignment

  • Make and test an embedded microcontroller system that you designed.

Extra credit (optional, as marked on the official page)

  • Make it with another process.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe toolpath generation, milling or laser engraving, stuffing, debugging and programming.
  • Demonstrate correct workflows and identify where they could improve.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • The group documents the machine settings for in-house PCB production, such as feeds, speeds, plunge rate, depth of cut and tooling.
  • For the board house, document the workflow up to the final order button; you do not have to place the order.
  • Show the board works by uploading a program to it.
  • Machine NC files are not required.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Documented how you made the toolpath.
  • Documented how you made the board (milled, stuffed, soldered).
  • Documented that your board works.
  • Explained any problems and how you fixed them.
  • Uploaded your source code.
  • Included a hero shot of your board.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A milled or engraved board you designed, stuffed and soldered, with the toolpath settings recorded.
  • A program uploaded to it, with a photo or video as proof.
  • The board-house submission walked through up to the order step.
  • Problems (for example a broken trace or a bridge) and how you fixed them.

Exceptional

  • The official extra: the board made with a second process, and the two compared.
  • A design-rules test cut that sets your minimum trace and gap, used in the board.
  • Close-up photos showing solder joints and how you inspected them.
  • A short debugging log with the multimeter checks you made.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Not checking for shorts between power and ground before plugging in.
  • Using a worn or wrong end mill and blaming the design.
  • No proof that the board runs code.
  • Forgetting to document the group's machine settings.
