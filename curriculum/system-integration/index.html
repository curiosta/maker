What the official assignment asks for

Designing for manufacture, packaging, wiring, power, testing, failure modes and repair.

Individual assignment

  • Design and document the system integration for your final project.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Define and apply system integration to your final project.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • Packaging means PCBs are mounted, wires are soldered or use proper connectors and are laid out neatly, and every part has its own place.
  • 'Looks like a finished product' means more than a simple laser-cut box.
  • It is assessed with your final project documentation, so link to it from the final project page.
  • The plan can be CAD models, system diagrams, cardboard mock-ups and so on.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Made a plan for system integration.
  • Documented the plan with CAD and/or sketches.
  • Implemented methods of packaging.
  • Designed the final project to look like a finished product.
  • Documented the system integration.
  • Linked to it from your final project page.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A system diagram and a CAD model or mock-up showing where each part goes.
  • Boards mounted, connectors used, wiring routed and secured.
  • Power, cables and access for charging or repair thought through.
  • The integration page linked from your final project page.

Exceptional

  • Design for manufacture and repair: parts that can be removed and replaced, labelled connectors.
  • Testing and failure modes considered, with what you did about them.
  • An enclosure that looks and feels designed, combining several processes.
  • Photos from mock-up to finished product.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Leaving integration to the last week.
  • A box with loose boards inside.
  • No space left for cables, connectors or a battery.
  • Forgetting to link it from the final project page.
