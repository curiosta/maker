What the official assignment asks for

Individual assignment

  • Design and produce something with a digital process (incorporating computer-aided design and manufacturing) not covered in another assignment, documenting the requirements that your assignment meets, and including everything necessary to reproduce it.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate workflows used in the chosen process.
  • Select and apply suitable processes and materials.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • Manual steps are allowed, but the workflow must include digital design, fabrication or computation.
  • If you choose composites, the group work of making test coupons is required.
  • You do not have to make something tangible; replicable digital processes such as embedded AI count.
  • Three-axis machining counts unless you already did it; then aim for smooth tilted or curved surfaces rather than steps.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Documented the workflows and processes you used.
  • Explained why your process is not covered in other assignments.
  • Described problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included original design files and source code.
  • Included a hero shot of the result.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • One digital process not used elsewhere in the course, with the requirements it meets written down.
  • Files and settings that let someone reproduce it.
  • Problems and fixes described.

Exceptional

  • A process chosen because your final project needs it, with that link explained.
  • Test pieces or coupons that explore the settings before the final piece.
  • A clear comparison with the process you might otherwise have used.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Choosing a process already covered, such as a plain laser cut.
  • No digital step in the workflow.
  • Missing files needed to reproduce the result.
