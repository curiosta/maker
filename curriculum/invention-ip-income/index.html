What the official assignment asks for

Invention, patents, copyright, licenses, trademarks and business models.

Individual assignment

  • Develop a plan for dissemination of your final project.
  • Complete your final project, tracking your progress: what tasks have been completed and what remain, what is working and what is not, what questions need to be resolved, what will happen when, and what you have learned.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Develop a plan to share your work.
  • Formulate future opportunities or development for your final project.
  • Summarise and communicate the essence of your project.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • A dissemination plan is still needed if the project is only for your own use; it can be about who it is for, how it might be funded and which licence you choose.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Created a dissemination plan for your final project.
  • Outlined future possibilities and how to make them probabilities.
  • Which tasks are done and which remain?
  • What is working and what is not?
  • What questions still need resolving?
  • What will happen when?
  • What have you learned?

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • A licence chosen for your project, with the reason.
  • A short plan for who could use it and how they would find it.
  • The progress questions answered honestly.

Exceptional

  • Separate licences considered for hardware, software and documentation.
  • A realistic look at funding or a business model, with sources.
  • Prior art or patents checked for the core idea, with what you found.
  • A next-version roadmap with concrete steps.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Choosing a licence without reading what it allows.
  • Mixing code and hardware under a licence meant for only one of them.
  • Treating the progress questions as a formality.
