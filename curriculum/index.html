Curriculum

What does finished look like, module by module?

An unofficial companion to the Fab Academy curriculum. For each of the 19 modules: what the official assignment asks for and how it is assessed, a documentation checklist, what bare-minimum and exceptional work could look like, common pitfalls, open tools, and projects on this site that practise it.

Applies to every module

The rules behind every assignment

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment guide, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. The official pages are the authority.

General essentials

  • Keep your original, editable design files in your class archive (your GitLab repository), not on outside hosting. Talk to your instructor about any file over 10 MB; the final project video can be up to 25 MB.
  • Compress images and video before you commit them.
  • A 'hero shot' is a photo or video of the finished result.
  • Acknowledge work by others, human or machine. If you used AI, document the prompts you used.
  • Commercial boards follow the Commercial Board Policy: they are not evaluated as your own work.
  • Documentation is in English.
  • Students continuing from an earlier year are assessed against the current year's standards.

Source: General essentials. Every student also signs the student agreement.

Commercial board policy

  • Commercial microcontroller boards are not evaluated as your design or production work.
  • Arduino-type boards are accepted only as extra nodes, for example in networking, alongside a board you made.
  • A single-board computer can stand in for a laptop (for example as the host for an interface).

Source: Commercial board policy.

Midterm review

  • Documentation of the assignments so far is reviewed.
  • Show progress on your final project idea and planning, with a system diagram, a task list and a schedule for the remaining work.
  • Meet with your local and global instructors.

Source: Midterm review.

Final project requirements

  • A summary slide (presentation.png, 1920x1080) and a roughly one-minute video (presentation.mp4, 1080p, up to about 25 MB) in the root of your site.
  • A separate final project page, a bill of materials, links to the weeks that fed into it, how you did system integration, the licence you chose, and acknowledgements.
  • Your own microcontroller PCB with an input and an output, 2D and 3D design, and additive and subtractive processes. A milled PCB counts as electronics production, not as the subtractive process.
  • A shield for a commercial board does not meet the requirement, and a working tangle of wires is not enough integration.

Source: Final project requirements.

Returning students

Coming back after a break

Life happens. If you paused the course and want to pick it up again, here is a calm order of work. These steps are the author's suggestions for getting your bearings, not official Fab Academy policy. What your lab and instructor accept is their decision.

  1. Find out what applies to you

    This site cannot tell you what your lab or instructor will accept after a break, or what your deadlines are. Ask your instructor or lab, and keep their answer in your own notes.

  2. Use the current cohort's pages

    Fab Academy publishes its schedule and class pages per year. Assignments can change between years, so check the current year's pages on fabacademy.org before you rely on notes from an earlier cohort, including the dates and wording on this site, which come from the 2026 cohort.

  3. Take stock of what you already have

    Open your documentation site and repository and list, module by module, what exists and what is missing. Read each official assignment bullet on the module pages here and note which one has evidence and which does not.

  4. Check your tools still work

    Make sure you can still log in, commit and publish your documentation, and that your software and files open. Fix this first, because everything else depends on it.

  5. Order the remaining work

    As a suggestion (the author's, not an official rule): finish the modules that your final project depends on first. Electronics design, electronics production, input devices, output devices, networking and interface work build on each other, and system integration needs all of them.

  6. Document the gap honestly

    Say what you did and when, including the break. Your documentation is for readers who were not there, and an honest timeline is more useful to them than a tidy one.

  7. Re-plan your final project

    Read the final project requirements on the applications and implications page, and check that your plan still fits the time you have. Cutting scope early is better than cutting corners late.
