What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Use the test equipment in your lab to observe the operation of an embedded microcontroller.

Individual assignment

  • Use an EDA tool to design an embedded microcontroller system using parts from the inventory, and check its design rules for fabrication.

Extra credit (optional, as marked on the official page)

  • Simulate a circuit.
  • Try another design workflow.
  • Design a case.

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Select and use software for circuit board design.
  • Demonstrate workflows used in circuit board design.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • For the group work, the minimum is to demonstrate a logic analyzer; other test equipment is welcome.
  • Draw your board from scratch; modifying an existing EDA project does not count.
  • A hand drawing is allowed, but you still have to show the use of EDA software.
  • 'Interact and communicate' means a development board with local input and/or output, able to talk to an external device.
  • If your EDA tool has no design rules check, you can generate toolpaths in mods and inspect the traces.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Documented what you learned in electronics design.
  • Checked your board can be fabricated.
  • Explained problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included the original design files (for example KiCad).
  • Included a hero shot.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • Group work: a documented observation of an embedded microcontroller using your lab's test equipment, with at least one photo or screenshot and a sentence on what it shows. State clearly what you did yourself.
  • A schematic and a board layout made in an EDA tool, with the design files linked (source files, not only screenshots).
  • Every part comes from your lab's inventory, and you list them (a simple bill of materials is enough).
  • The design rules check was run against the rules for your fabrication process, and you show the result.
  • A short explanation of what each important part of the circuit is for (power, programming header, reset, clock and the pins you used).

Exceptional

  • Any of the official extras done properly: a simulated circuit with the results explained, a second design workflow compared with the first, or a case designed for the board.
  • The board is designed for your final project, with decisions explained: alternatives you considered, datasheet references behind pin and power choices, and a rough power budget.
  • Design rules tuned to your lab's actually characterized process rather than defaults, with the trace widths and clearances justified.
  • Reproducible: custom footprints and libraries included, a BOM with part numbers, and a version history showing what the checks caught and what you changed.
  • A short reflection on mistakes and what you would do differently on the next revision.

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Starting from someone's existing board file.
  • Running the design rules check with default rules rather than your lab's process.
  • Forgetting a programming header, reset or power indication.
  • Choosing parts your lab does not stock. Check the inventory first.
