What the official assignment asks for

Group assignment

  • Send a message between two projects.

Individual assignment

  • Design, build and connect wired or wireless node(s) with network or bus addresses and local input and/or output device(s).

How it is assessed

Paraphrased

From the Fab Academy 2026 assessment page on fabacademy.org, retrieved 29 Oct 2026. Your local and global instructors decide what passes.

Learning outcomes

  • Demonstrate workflows used in network design.
  • Implement and interpret networking and/or communication protocols.

Clarified in the official FAQ

  • 'Two projects' in the group work can be any mix of boards, computers or phones, commercial ones included, but you write the code that sends or receives the message.
  • You can reuse boards you made in earlier weeks.
  • Commercial boards are allowed if at least one board is your own design; commercial wireless modules are fine if integrated into your design.
  • Each node needs a unique address used to select it; you need at least two boards.

All modules also follow the general essentials (original files in your archive, compressed media, acknowledgements, AI prompts documented) and the commercial board policy. Summary of both.

Documentation checklist

The official "have you answered these questions?" list for this module, paraphrased. Tick each one off in your documentation.

  • Linked to the group assignment page.
  • Documented what you learned implementing networking or communication protocols.
  • Explained the programming process you used.
  • Made sure, and documented, that addressing your boards works.
  • Outlined problems and how you fixed them.
  • Included design files (or links to earlier boards) and original source code.
  • Included a hero shot of your network setup.

Bare-minimum vs exceptional

Author's interpretation

Author's interpretation, not official Fab Academy grading. Use it to aim your work and check your documentation, and confirm what counts with your instructor.

Bare-minimum

  • At least two nodes, one of them your own board, exchanging messages over a wired bus or a wireless link.
  • Addressing shown: you send to one node and only that node responds.
  • Code explained, with design files and source included.

Exceptional

  • Three or more nodes, with what happens when one fails or is removed.
  • Signals inspected with a logic analyzer or oscilloscope and decoded.
  • A small message format you designed, explained and versioned.
  • Two protocols compared for your final project (for example I2C versus UART, or wired versus wireless).

Common pitfalls

The author's notes, partly drawn from the official FAQ. Not official grading.

  • Two boards that only talk point-to-point, with no addressing.
  • Missing pull-up resistors on I2C, or mismatched voltage levels.
  • No common ground on a wired link.
  • Using only commercial boards.
