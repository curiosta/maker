Twin-Lab LoRa Messenger (Build to a Shared Open Spec)

  • intermediate
  • 10-12 hours + a joint test session

SDG 17 · Partnerships for the Goals

Two Fab Labs agree an open packet spec, each build a node, and prove they interoperate, a small model of cooperation and technology sharing between partners (targets 17.6 and 17.16).

Safety notes

  • India: use the licence-exempt 865-867 MHz band (WPC de-licensed it for low-power devices within the power limits in its notification); keep the module's default low power, use the IN865 settings, and buy modules whose seller states WPC ETA compliance. Never use 433 or 915 MHz modules.
  • LiPo: use a cell with a protection circuit, check polarity before connecting to the XIAO BAT pads, never puncture, crush or short it, and stop using a swollen cell. An 18650 also works on the XIAO BAT pads: protected cell, correct polarity.

Draft and unofficial, not affiliated with Fab Academy or the United Nations. Check prices, GST and parts before you buy.

Summary

A handheld node with OLED and buttons sends short preset or typed messages over LoRa to a node built by another team or lab. Works with no internet; the shared spec and test results are published openly.

What it teaches

Write a short interface spec with a partner team (frequency plan, spreading factor, packet format, checksum), implement it independently, then run interoperability tests and fix the spec, not just the code. Document so a third lab could join.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Networking and communications
  • Embedded programming
  • Electronics production
  • Computer-aided design
  • Project development (documentation)
