Tamper-Detecting Smart Seal (ATtiny, Cast in Epoxy)

  • advanced
  • 16-20 hours over several days (milling, programming, casting)

SDG 16 · Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions

A low-cost seal that records if and when it was broken explores how technology can support accountability and evidence integrity (targets 16.5 and 16.6).

Safety notes

  • Epoxy: work ventilated, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection, pour thin layers to limit heat build-up.
  • Coin cells: only cast a CR2032 (non-rechargeable) and never a LiPo; keep spare coin cells away from children, as swallowed cells cause severe injury.
  • It is a teaching prototype, not a certified security seal.

Draft and unofficial, not affiliated with Fab Academy or the United Nations. Check prices, GST and parts before you buy.

Summary

Thread the enamelled-wire loop through a bag, box or cabinet latch. If the loop is cut, the seal records it and changes its blink pattern; reading the log over UPDI shows that it was opened. Discuss what attacks it resists and which it does not.

What it teaches

Design and mill an ATtiny board that watches a fine wire loop, sleeps at microamps on a coin cell, logs the first break (and a running count of wake-ups as a crude clock) to EEPROM, and blinks a status LED. Program over UPDI, then mould and cast the board in clear epoxy so the electronics cannot be reached without visible damage.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Electronics design
  • Electronics production
  • Embedded programming
  • Molding and casting
  • Computer-aided design
