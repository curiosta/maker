PIR Wildlife Camera Trap (ESP32-CAM, 3D-Printed Housing)

  • advanced
  • 10-14 hours

SDG 15 · Life on Land

Supports local biodiversity surveys on a campus, farm or village edge, the basic data behind protecting species and habitats (targets 15.5 and 15.7).

Safety notes

  • 18650 cells: use protected cells, set any boost/buck output with a multimeter before connecting the board, never short the cell, and do not leave charging unattended.
  • Point it at wildlife paths, not at people or neighbours' property. Inside protected areas or forest land, get permission from the forest department first.
  • PIR triggers on warm-bodied animals; it will miss most reptiles and insects.

Draft and unofficial, not affiliated with Fab Academy or the United Nations. Check prices, GST and parts before you buy.

Summary

Strap it to a tree or post: when a warm-bodied animal passes, it wakes, takes a short burst of photos and sleeps again. Review the card to see who visits at night.

What it teaches

Wake an ESP32-CAM from deep sleep on a PIR trigger, capture and save time-stamped JPEGs to microSD, budget power from one 18650, and design a weather-resistant printed housing with a clear window (optionally cast in epoxy).

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Computer-aided design
  • 3D scanning and printing
  • Input devices
  • Embedded programming
  • Molding and casting (optional lens window)
  • System integration
