LoRa Water-Quality Buoy (Temperature + Turbidity)

  • advanced
  • 14-18 hours over several days (print, seal, cure, test)

SDG 14 · Life Below Water

Lets students monitor temperature and turbidity in a lake, backwater or harbour edge, the kind of local data that supports reducing water pollution (target 14.1).

Safety notes

Read first. Radio band: in India, use the licence-exempt 865–867 MHz band. The LoRa module in the parts list has not been checked for that band yet, so confirm it on the module's datasheet before you buy one.

  • Water: never work at open water alone; wear a life jacket near deep or moving water; test in a tank first.
  • 18650 cells: use protected cells, set any boost/buck output with a multimeter before connecting the board, never short the cell, and do not leave charging unattended. Never charge the cell inside the sealed hull; add a desiccant pack.
  • Epoxy potting: ventilate, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection.
  • Radio: in India use the licence-exempt 865-867 MHz band (set the module accordingly). Retrieve the buoy and all parts after every test so nothing is left in the water.

Summary

A floating node reads water temperature and turbidity every 10 minutes and transmits over LoRa to a second XIAO on shore that logs and displays the data. Test in a tank, then a pond, before any open water.

What it teaches

Design a sealed, stable 3D-printed buoy (buoyancy and ballast maths, gland and gasket design, potting with epoxy), read temperature and turbidity, send packets over LoRa to a shore receiver, and budget power for multi-day deployment.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Computer-aided design
  • 3D scanning and printing
  • Molding and casting
  • Input devices
  • Networking and communications
  • Embedded programming
  • System integration
