3D-Printed Micro Wind Turbine with Power Logging

  • intermediate
  • 10-14 hours

SDG 7 · Affordable and Clean Energy

Shows honestly how much electricity a small turbine makes at real wind speeds, building intuition for renewable energy and its limits.

Safety notes

  • Spinning blades: test behind a guard, keep faces and fingers out of the rotor plane, and check blades for cracks before every run; printed blades can shed at high speed.
  • Mount outdoors only on a secure, low pole away from people; take it down in storms.

Draft and unofficial, not affiliated with Fab Academy or the United Nations. Check prices, GST and parts before you buy.

Summary

A desk-to-balcony turbine: test it in front of a table fan first, then outdoors. The OLED shows live milliwatts and the logger builds a power curve, so students can compare blade designs.

What it teaches

Design and print a 3-blade rotor (airfoil, pitch, balance), mount it on a DC motor used as a generator with a 608 bearing, and log voltage, current and power with an INA219 into a dummy load to plot a power curve.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Computer-aided design
  • 3D scanning and printing
  • Mechanical design
  • Input devices
  • Embedded programming
