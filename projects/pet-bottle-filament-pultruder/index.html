PET-Bottle Strip Filament Pultruder

  • advanced
  • 20-25 hours

SDG 12 · Responsible Consumption and Production

Turns used PET bottles into 3D-printing filament, a hands-on lesson in reuse and closing material loops in the lab.

Safety notes

  • Heat: the nozzle runs at around 200-230 C; use a guard, a silicone sock and a stand, and never leave it heated unattended.
  • Firmware must cut the heater on thermistor faults or runaway; add an inline thermal fuse as a last resort.
  • Blades: the strip cutter uses a fixed utility blade; shroud it, keep fingers behind the bottle, and wear cut-resistant gloves.
  • Fumes: ventilate or use extraction; do not overheat or burn PET.
  • Use only a certified external adapter; do not open, build or modify mains supplies.

Draft and unofficial, not affiliated with Fab Academy or the United Nations. Check prices, GST and parts before you buy.

Summary

Cut a clean bottle into a long strip, feed it through a hot nozzle, and a slow stepper pulls out roughly 1.75 mm filament onto a printed spool. Characterise diameter consistency and print test pieces; PET usually needs higher print temperatures than PLA.

What it teaches

Build a bottle strip cutter, then a pultruder: a hotend with a widened nozzle shapes the strip into round filament while a stepper winds it onto a spool. Implement PID temperature control with thermal-runaway protection and match pull speed to temperature.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • Mechanical design
  • Machine design
  • Output devices
  • Input devices
  • Embedded programming
  • 3D scanning and printing
