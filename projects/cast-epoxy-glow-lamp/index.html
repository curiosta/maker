Solar Cast Epoxy Lantern (3D-Printed Master, Silicone Mould, 18650 + Solar)

  • advanced
  • 12-16 hours over 2-3 days (cure times)

SDG 7 · Affordable and Clean Energy

A solar-charged, battery-powered cast lantern that teaches energy budgeting: how much light a small panel and one cell can really deliver off-grid.

Safety notes

  • Epoxy and silicone: work ventilated, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection, follow the supplier's mix ratio by weight.
  • Do not cast electronics or the battery into epoxy; keep them in a removable, ventilated base (heat, venting and repairability).
  • 18650 cells: use protected cells, set any boost/buck output with a multimeter before connecting the board, never short the cell, and do not leave charging unattended.
  • Keep the solar panel's open-circuit voltage within the TP4056 module's input rating; add a series diode if the panel can back-feed.

Summary

An off-grid lantern: the diffuser is cast in a silicone mould taken from your own 3D-printed master. The base holds an 18650 cell, a solar charger and a 12-LED ring; the panel charges the cell by day and doubles as a dusk sensor. A button cycles brightness, and an optional Wi-Fi page sets the colour. Measure how many hours of light a sunny day buys.

What it teaches

Design a diffuser dome with a cavity for the electronics, print the master, make a two-part silicone mould (parting line, pour/vent design, release), cast in clear epoxy and finish it (sand to frost). Then power a WS2812 ring from XIAO ESP32-C3 off one 18650 cell charged by a small solar panel through a protected TP4056, boost to 5 V, sense battery and panel voltage, and switch on at dusk.

Topics it touches, in Fab Academy-style terms:

  • 3D scanning and printing
  • Molding and casting
  • Output devices
  • Embedded programming
  • Networking and communications
  • Computer-aided design
